1.  Executive summary
7 Fifteen Capital requires a modern platform to run facilities, estates and residential communities end-to-end. Unrealabs
proposes a 2-in-1, AI-powered platform that unifies facility operations and estate/community management on a single
shared core — predicting problems before they happen, automating the busywork, and giving managers, residents,
owners and investors one modern experience across mobile, web and admin.
The product ships as a multi-tenant platform with two functional modules — Facility Management and Estate
Management — on a shared, AI-enabled core covering payments, multi-platform apps, document intelligence and an
investor off-plan dashboard. Delivery is a one-time build priced at USD 300,000 over an indicative ≈24–26 weeks (July
– December 2026).
What changed in v2.0: this revision makes the PRD buildable. Every requirement now carries a priority (MoSCoW), a
user story and testable acceptance criteria; the domain model, tenancy model, permissions matrix and key workflow
states are defined; NFRs are quantified; AI features specify inputs/outputs, human-in-the-loop behaviour, fallbacks and
proposed benchmarks; and an open-questions register assigns the decisions still needed before or during discovery.
Items marked (proposed) or (TBC) are baselines for estimation and build — they are confirmed with 7 Fifteen Capital at
discovery and do not silently become contract terms.

3.  Problem & opportunity
Management today is fragmented and largely manual — spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups, paper logs and disconnected
tools. The predictable results:
 Reactive, not preventive — assets fail before anyone acts, driving up downtime and cost.
 Slow request handling — issues get lost; the right technician or vendor is hard to assign.
 Leaky billing — service charges go unpaid and reconciliation is painful.
 Poor experience — little transparency, slow communication, no self-service.
 No single truth — data is scattered, so reporting and decisions are guesswork.
The opportunity: one intelligent platform running both facility operations and estate/community life on a shared core —
problems predicted rather than reacted to, routine work automated, and every stakeholder working from the same live
data.

8.2  Tenancy model & isolation requirements
 Hierarchy: Platform → Tenant (organisation) → Estates/Facilities → Buildings → Units → Residents/Assets. Every
domain record carries its tenant scope.
 Hard isolation: no query, file, search index, AI retrieval or report may cross tenants below Platform Admin. Verified
by automated cross-tenant access tests in CI (release-blocking).
 Isolation strategy: shared database with tenant-scoped rows enforced at application and database layer (e.g.
row-level security), tenant-scoped object storage and per-tenant vector-store namespaces (proposed — confirm as
Architecture Decision ADR-01, open question Q2).
 Tenant onboarding: Platform Admin creates the organisation, then configures estates/units (bulk import), branding,
payment settlement account, languages and enabled features.
 Per-tenant configuration: branding, notification templates, amenity rules, charge structures, AI feature flags and
usage caps.
 Cost metering: AI, SMS and email usage metered per tenant (assumption §15: usage costs are billed to the Client).

11.4  AI guardrails & design requirements (release-blocking)
 Tenant isolation: retrieval, embeddings and context are hard-scoped to the tenant; automated cross-tenant leakage
tests run in CI.
 Provider abstraction: LLM/OCR/translation behind an abstraction with health checks; on provider failure, features
degrade to their listed fallback — core flows never block on AI.
 Cost controls: per-tenant and per-feature usage caps with alerts and kill-switch per feature (usage billed to Client
per §15).
 Prompt-injection defence: user- and document-supplied content is treated as data; the assistant cannot be
instructed by it to exceed the user’s permissions (§7).
 Logging & privacy: all AI interactions logged (AI interaction log, §8.1) with PII minimised/redacted in logs.
 UX states: every AI surface shows loading, confidence, sources and error states; every suggestion is
review/approve/override — AI is assistive, not autonomous.
